# References

1. L. C. Freeman, *Centrality in social networks: Conceptual clarification*,
   Social Networks 1(3), 215–239, 1978/79 — the group centralization index
   and degree centrality. `@Freeman1979`
2. M. E. J. Newman, *Networks: An Introduction*, Oxford University Press, 2010 —
   connectivity, degree centrality, and network-efficiency measures.
   `@Newman2010`
3. U. Brandes, *A faster algorithm for betweenness centrality*, Journal of
   Mathematical Sociology 25(2), 163–177, 2001 — betweenness via BFS
   predecessor counting. `@Brandes2001`
4. R. E. Tarjan, *Depth-first search and linear graph algorithms*, SIAM J.
   Computing 1(2), 146–160, 1972 — articulation (critical) points via
   low-link DFS. `@Tarjan1972`
5. J. Hopcroft, R. E. Tarjan, *Algorithm 447: Efficient algorithms for graph
   manipulation*, Communications of the ACM 16(6), 372–378, 1973 — the
   biconnectivity/articulation implementation this module follows.
   `@HopcroftTarjan1973`
6. V. Latora, M. Marchiori, *Efficient behavior of small-world networks*,
   Physical Review Letters 87(19), 198701, 2001 — global efficiency as the
   mean inverse shortest-path length. `@Latora2001`
7. R. Albert, H. Jeong, A.-L. Barabási, *Error and attack tolerance of complex
   networks*, Nature 406, 378–382, 2000 — targeted removal of hubs fragments a
   centralized network far faster than random failure; the framing behind the
   robustness curve and node-removal impact. `@Albert2000`
8. S. P. Borgatti, *Identifying sets of key players in a social network*,
   Computational and Mathematical Organization Theory 12(1), 21–34, 2006 —
   the key-player formulation behind the greedy critical-node ordering.
   `@Borgatti2006`
9. A. O. Hirschman, *The paternity of an index*, The American Economic Review
   54(5), 761–762, 1964 — the Herfindahl–Hirschman concentration index used
   for flow concentration. `@Hirschman1964`
10. D. Kempe, J. Kleinberg, É. Tardos, *Maximizing the spread of influence
    through a social network*, KDD '03, 137–146, 2003 — the independent-cascade
    model underlying both cascade layers, and the submodularity of
    coverage-style influence objectives. `@Kempe2003`
11. M. E. J. Newman, *Spread of epidemic disease on networks*, Physical Review
    E 66, 016128, 2002 — percolation-style spread motivating the compromise
    cascade. `@Newman2002`
12. G. L. Nemhauser, L. A. Wolsey, M. L. Fisher, *An analysis of approximations
    for maximizing submodular set functions—I*, Mathematical Programming
    14(1), 265–294, 1978 — the `(1 - 1/e)` greedy guarantee for budgeted
    submodular interception coverage. `@Nemhauser1978`
13. J. Leskovec, A. Krause, C. Guestrin, C. Faloutsos, J. VanBriesen,
    N. Glance, *Cost-effective outbreak detection in networks*, KDD '07,
    420–429, 2007 — submodular sensor placement, the closest published analogue
    of the monitoring-allocation problem. `@Leskovec2007`
14. D. S. Johnson, *Approximation algorithms for combinatorial problems*,
    JCSS 9(3), 256–278, 1974 — the greedy set-cover heuristic.
    `@Johnson1974`
15. U. Feige, *A threshold of ln n for approximating set cover*, J. ACM 45(4),
    634–652, 1998 — the logarithmic approximation lower bound for set cover.
    `@Feige1998`
16. *Spectre* (2015), Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer / Columbia Pictures — source of the
    Nine Eyes programme and the SPECTRE brotherhood conceit.
